  5. 4. Deviations

The interrexx Language Specification

BITOR

BITOR(string1 [, string2] [, pad])

ANSI: 9.6.3; rexxref: Functions > Built-in Functions > BITOR (Bit by Bit OR)

Deviations: D4

BITOR combines two strings bit by bit, setting a bit wherever either string has it set, and answers the string of the combined characters. How the two strings are lined up and what happens when their lengths differ are the rules of the bit-by-bit functions, shared with BITAND and BITXOR.

Arguments

[bif.bitor.method]

BITOR is a builtin that runs as a String method, and string1, its first argument, is the receiver; string2 and pad become the message's arguments, in that order. Its arguments, their checks and their defaults are those of the bit-by-bit functions: string1 is required, string2 defaults to the null string, and pad has its own check and no default.

say c2x(bitor('f0'x, '3c'x)) c2x('f0'x~bitor('3c'x)) c2x('f0'x~bitor('3c'x, '00'x))
signal on syntax name a
say bitor('a', 'b', 'xy')
a: say condition('o')~code

FC FC FC
40.23

The result

[bif.bitor.result]

A bit of the answer is set when the corresponding bit of either operand, or of both, is set, and clear otherwise, for every character of the answer that both operands have. The characters that only the longer operand has follow the rules for unequal lengths and for a pad.

say c2x(bitor('f0'x, '3c'x)) c2x(bitor('00'x, '5a'x)) c2x(bitor('ff'x, '5a'x))
say c2x(bitor('12'x || '34'x, 'f0'x || '0f'x)) c2x(bitor('a1'x, '1a'x))

FC 5A FF
F23F BB
[bif.bitor.mask]

A character ORed with '00'x is unchanged, and one ORed with 'FF'x becomes 'FF'x. So a pad of '00'x keeps the characters of the longer operand that lie past the end of the shorter, and a pad of 'FF'x sets every bit of them.

say c2x(bitor('abc', 'a', '00'x)) c2x(bitor('abc', 'a', 'ff'x))
say bitor('abc', , '00'x) c2x(bitor('abc', , 'ff'x))

616263 61FFFF
abc FFFFFF

Deviations

[bif.bitor.deviation.4]

interrexx does not specify whether the answer of BITOR is the same object as another string of equal value, as the shared rule says for every bit-by-bit function. A program can rely only on the value, which is equal under ==.

say (bitor('ab', 'ab') == 'ab') (bitor('x', , '00'x) == 'x')
