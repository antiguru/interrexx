The interrexx Language Specification

FILESPEC

FILESPEC(option, filespec)

rexxref: Functions > Built-in Functions > FILESPEC

FILESPEC takes a file specification apart and answers one piece of it: its directory, its file name or the extension of that name. It works on the characters of the specification alone. It looks at no file, so the file need not exist, and it never asks the file system anything.

The rules here are those of a Unix-like system, where the components of a specification are separated by slashes. The specifications of other platforms, such as those with a drive letter or with backslashes, are not specified.

Arguments

[bif.filespec.args]

option is a string whose first character picks the piece, as the next rule says, and filespec is a string; both are required. FILESPEC does not run as a String method: sending the message FILESPEC to a string raises SYNTAX 97.1. It follows the native call rule, both for the number of arguments and for an argument that is an object other than a string, such as .nil.

signal on syntax name a
say filespec()
a: say condition('o')~code
signal on syntax name b
say filespec('N')
b: say condition('o')~code
signal on syntax name c
say filespec(, 'a/b')
c: say condition('o')~code
signal on syntax name d
say filespec('N', 'a/b', 'c')
d: say condition('o')~code
signal on syntax name e
say filespec('N', .nil)
e: say condition('o')~code
signal on syntax name f
say 'a/b'~filespec('N')
f: say condition('o')~code
signal on syntax name g
say filespec('N', .told~new)
g: say condition('o')~code
signal off syntax
say filespec('N', 'a/b', ) filespec(.array~of('N'), .mutablebuffer~new('a/b'))

[bif.filespec.option]

The first character of option picks the piece, in either case, and the characters after it are ignored. The pieces are Drive, Extension, Location, Name and Path, so the first character is one of D, E, L, N and P. Any other first character, and the null string, raises SYNTAX 40.904. A blank is a character: it does not pick a piece, and nothing strips it. The checks of the arguments come first, so a call with a wrong option and a wrong argument raises the error of the argument.

f = '/usr/lib/libm.so'
say filespec('Name', f) filespec('NAMEXYZ', f) filespec('n', f)
say filespec('path', f) filespec('Location', f) filespec('P', f)
say '[' || filespec('drive', f) || ']' filespec('ext', f) filespec('e', f)
signal on syntax name a
say filespec('', f)
a: say condition('o')~code
signal on syntax name b
say filespec('X', f)
b: say condition('o')~code
signal on syntax name c
say filespec(' N', f)
c: say condition('o')~code
signal on syntax name d
say filespec('X', .nil)
d: say condition('o')~code
signal on syntax name e
say filespec('X', f, 'x')
e: say condition('o')~code
signal on syntax name g
say filespec('X')

The pieces

[bif.filespec.path]

Path is the part of filespec up to and including its last slash, and the null string when filespec has no slash. Location is the same string. A specification that ends in a slash is all path, and one that is only slashes is its own path.

say '[' || filespec('P', '/usr/lib/libm.so') || ']' '[' || filespec('L', 'a/b') || ']'
say '[' || filespec('P', 'libm.so') || ']' '[' || filespec('L', 'libm.so') || ']'
say '[' || filespec('P', '/usr/lib/') || ']' '[' || filespec('P', 'a/b/') || ']'
say '[' || filespec('P', '/') || ']' '[' || filespec('L', '//') || ']'
say '[' || filespec('P', '/x') || ']' '[' || filespec('P', 'a//b') || ']'

[bif.filespec.name]

Name is the part of filespec after its last slash. It is the whole of filespec when there is no slash, and the null string when filespec ends in a slash. A name of periods, such as . or .., is a name like another. A '00'x ends filespec: every piece is taken from the characters before the first '00'x, and the characters from it on are part of no piece. A line feed, a tab and every other character are ordinary characters. The path and the name together are filespec up to its first '00'x, with nothing lost between them.

say '[' || filespec('N', '/usr/lib/libm.so') || ']' '[' || filespec('N', 'libm.so') || ']'
say '[' || filespec('N', '/usr/lib/') || ']' '[' || filespec('N', '/') || ']'
say '[' || filespec('N', '.') || ']' '[' || filespec('N', '/x/..') || ']'
say '[' || filespec('N', 'x y/z w') || ']' '[' || filespec('P', 'x y/z w') || ']'
r = ''
do s over .array~of('/a/b.c', 'a//b', '/', '', 'a/', ' a/b ')
  r = r (filespec('P', s) || filespec('N', s) == s)
end
say strip(r)
s = 'x/y.v' || '00'x || '/z.w'

say filespec('N', 'a/b' || '0a'x || 'c') == 'b' || '0a'x || 'c'

[bif.filespec.extension]

Extension is the part of the name after its last period. It is the null string when the name has no period and when its last period is its last character. A period that starts the name counts like any other, so a name that is only an extension has it as its extension. Periods in the path before the name play no part.

say '[' || filespec('E', 'libm.so') || ']' '[' || filespec('E', 'a.b.c') || ']'
say '[' || filespec('E', 'archive.tar.gz') || ']' '[' || filespec('E', '/x/.hidden') || ']'
say '[' || filespec('E', 'noext') || ']' '[' || filespec('E', 'dot.') || ']'
say '[' || filespec('E', '/a.b/c') || ']' '[' || filespec('E', 'a.b/') || ']'
say '[' || filespec('E', '/a/b.c.') || ']' '[' || filespec('E', '..x') || ']'
say '[' || filespec('E', 'x/b.c d') || ']' '[' || filespec('E', '.') || ']'

[bif.filespec.drive]

Drive is the null string, whatever filespec is: a Unix-like system has no drives, and a colon or a letter that looks like a drive is part of the name.

say '[' || filespec('D', '/usr/lib') || ']' '[' || filespec('D', 'c:/x.txt') || ']'
say '[' || filespec('D', 'C:\win\f.txt') || ']' '[' || filespec('D', '') || ']'
say filespec('N', 'c:/x.txt') filespec('N', 'C:\win\f.txt')

[bif.filespec.null]

The null string has no pieces: every option answers the null string for it. A specification that is a number or has blanks in it is a specification like any other, taken by its string value.

r = ''
do o over .array~of('D', 'E', 'L', 'N', 'P')
  r = r || '[' || filespec(o, '') || ']'
end
say strip(r)
say filespec('N', 12) filespec('E', 1.5) filespec('N', 'a b.c d')
