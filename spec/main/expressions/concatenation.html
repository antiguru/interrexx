The interrexx Language Specification

Concatenation

ANSI: 7.4.6; rexxref: General Concepts > Terms, Expressions, and Operators > String Concatenation

Concatenation joins two values into one string. Rexx has three forms of it, which differ in what they put between the two values: the blank operator puts one blank, and the abuttal and the operator || put nothing. The blank is the form a program uses without noticing, as when it writes say 'total:' x. This page says when two terms are concatenated, what the joined string holds, and how concatenation ranks among the other operators.

[expr.concatenation.blank]

Two terms that are separated by one or more blanks or tabs, with no operator between them, are concatenated with exactly one blank in the result. The blanks of the program are not kept, and a tab becomes a blank, but the blanks that belong to the values are: a value that ends in a blank or begins with one keeps it. The null string takes part like any other value, so a blank remains on its side of the result. A blank next to an operator is no concatenation, as the blank rule says.

say 'a'      'b'	'c'
say 'a ' 'b' ' c'
say '[' '' 'a' '' ']'

a b c
a  b  c
[  a  ]
[expr.concatenation.abuttal]

Two terms with nothing between them, or with only a comment between them, are concatenated without a blank. This is the abuttal. It joins a string and a symbol, as in x'%', two parenthesized terms, and the value of a function call with the term after it. A comment is no blank, so it does not stop the abuttal.

x = 5
say x'%' x'<' (x)(x) x/**/x
say abs(-2)abs(-3) (1 + 2)(3)
say x /**/ x

5% 5< 55 55
23 33
5 5
[expr.concatenation.operator]

The operator || concatenates the two terms with no blank, and blanks around it are dropped. A line end ends the clause even after the operator, so a || at the end of a line without a continuation leaves it without a right operand and raises SYNTAX 35.1. The second example shows that in a program file, which exits with 256 minus the error number, and the third shows it in the text of an INTERPRET instruction, where the end of the text ends the clause and the error is an ordinary condition.

say 'a' || 'b' 'c'||'d'
say 'a' ||,
    'b'

ab cd
ab

say 'before'
say 'a' ||

signal on syntax name e
interpret "say 'a' ||"
e: say condition('o')~code

35.1
[expr.concatenation.precedence]

The three forms are on one level of precedence, below the arithmetic operators and above the comparisons. The operands of an arithmetic operator are therefore evaluated before the result is joined to a neighbour, and the concatenations of a run are applied from left to right.

say 1+2 3*4
say 1 + 1 || 2 * 3
say (1 || 2 = 12) (1 2 = '1 2')

3 12
26
1 1
[expr.concatenation.operands]

The operands are joined as they are, so a number is not put into its normal form, and 1.50 || 2.0 is 1.502.0. An operand that is an object other than a string is replaced by a string first, on either side of the operator, by the conversion rule. The result is a String also when an operand is an instance of a subclass of String, and that operand contributes its own characters.

say 1.50 || 2.0 007 || 7
arr = .array~of(1, 2)
say ('x' arr) ('x' .both~new) (.both~new 'x') (.nil 'x')
say ('x' .strsub~new('sub'))~class~id
signal on syntax name e
say 'x' .hidden~new
e: say condition('o')~code

97.2
[expr.concatenation.sign]

A blank before + or - does not start a concatenation, because the sign is an infix operator there and the blank next to it has no effect. To join a signed number to the term before it with a blank, the number goes in parentheses, and || takes it directly.

say (3 -1) (3 - 1) (3 (-1)) (3 || -1)

2 2 3 -1 3-1
